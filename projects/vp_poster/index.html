Plasma potential control using an emissive cathode

Poster on the hot cathode as an innovative plasma control tool.

Side view of a high-density plasma core generated by a hot cathode in an ambient radiofrequency plasma. An emissive probe (glowing filament) measures plasma potential along the column radius.

Plasma potential is a fundamental, spontaneous and hardly controllable property of plasmas. It induces an electric field E, which impacts the dynamics of magnetized plasmas through the ExB drift. Its control is thus of prime importance for reliable operation in plasma technologies.

We investigated the influence of current injection in a magnetized plasma column on plasma potential. The large dataset was leveraged to conduct an in-depth comparison with a theoretical model based on voltage drop computation using Ohm’s law in this anisotropic medium. It resulted in a good understanding of the radial voltage drop, paving the way for predictable plasma potential control using a hot cathode.

This poster is accompanied by a publication in Plasma Sources Science and Technology (Pagaud et al., 2025). It was presented at MagNetUS 2022 (online), and EPS Plasma 2022 (online), where I was awarded Best poster presentation.

    Plasma potential control in a magnetized plasma column using a thermionic cathode
    F Pagaud, V Dolique, S Vincent, B Trotabas, R Gueroult, and N Plihon
    Plasma Sources Science and Technology 34 6, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib PDF

    The ability to drive the plasma potential in a magnetized plasma column using strongly negatively biased emissive cathode is experimentally demonstrated. By analysing an extensive experimental dataset, and in particular the evolution of the radial profiles of the plasma parameters (plasma density, electron temperature, plasma potential) obtained for various background magnetic field and neutral filling pressure, we first show that current injection from a negatively biased cathode offers an additional level of control as compared to a cold cathode. The radial potential drop across the plasma column, i.e. perpendicular to the magnetic field, is observed to be proportional to the current injected at the cathode. The experimental observations are in excellent qualitative agreement with recent theoretical predictions (Trotabas and Gueroult 2022 Plasma Sources Sci. Technol. 31 025001), in particular for the scaling B^2/n_n of the plasma potential drop with magnetic field B and neutral filling pressure nn. However, the experimental dataset also reveals the existence of a strong electron sheath (up to a few times the electron temperature) at the outer edge of the plasma column, which is not currently included in the modelling.
